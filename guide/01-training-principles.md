# 01. Training principles: volume, frequency, intensity, effort, rest, and the maintenance dose

Last updated: 2026-10-21

This chapter states each training variable as a rule and gives the evidence behind it. Strength outcomes (1RM on the main lifts) and hypertrophy outcomes (muscle size) are kept separate throughout, because they respond differently to volume, load, and frequency.

Source note: all evidence comes from `research/frequency-volume-intensity-evidence.md` (cited below as FVI, Q1-Q7) and `research/flexible-frequency-programming.md` (FFP, KQ3). The research notes were compiled from abstracts and reputable secondary summaries because journal pages were blocked; numbers that came only from a secondary summary are marked **(secondary, unverified)** here exactly as they are in the notes. Pre-2016 work is marked **(older)**.

Population caveat: most meta-analyses pool mostly untrained or recreationally trained subjects. Where a trained-subject trial exists (Schoenfeld 2019 MSSE, Brigatto 2022, Aube 2022, Hamarsland 2022, Colquhoun 2018, Enes 2024, Räntilä 2026), weight it more heavily than the pooled effect sizes.

## 1. Volume: hard sets per muscle per week

### Rules

1. Count **hard sets** (sets taken to 0-3 reps in reserve). Warm-ups do not count.
2. For hypertrophy, target **10-20 hard sets per muscle per week**. Use the lower half when the week is short and the upper half when it is long (table below).
3. For strength on a main lift, **2-6 heavy direct sets per lift per session, 2-3 sessions per week** captures most of the signal. More sets build muscle, not 1RM.
4. Count indirect work at **half credit**: a pressing set counts ~0.5 for triceps and front delts; a row or pulldown set counts ~0.5 for biceps and rear delts (FVI Q2, Pelland's "fractional" counting).
5. Reserve 20+ weekly sets for a lagging muscle or a short overreaching block. Trained-lifter trials at 30-60 sets per week show small extra strength returns at best and, in one case, a deadlift penalty.

### Weekly set targets per muscle group

These numbers are the program's binding targets (design spine item 3). They are deliberately the same across muscle groups: the literature does not support muscle-by-muscle precision (FVI Q3). The fractional-counting column shows why small muscles need fewer *direct* sets to reach the same total.

| Muscle group | 3-day week (sets/week) | 4-day week | 5-6-day week | Sets per session | Exposures per week | Fractional credit from compounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chest | 8-12 | 10-14 | 14-20 | 3-5 (3-day) / 5-8 (5-6-day) | 2 (3-day) / 2-3 (5-6-day) | None needed; presses are direct |
| Back (lats, mid-back) | 8-12 | 10-14 | 14-20 | 3-5 / 5-8 | 2 / 2-3 | Rows and pulldowns are direct |
| Quads | 8-12 | 10-14 | 14-20 | 3-5 / 5-8 | 2 / 2-3 | Squats are direct |
| Hamstrings and glutes | 8-12 | 10-14 | 14-20 | 3-5 / 5-8 | 2 / 2-3 | Deadlift variants count for both |
| Shoulders (side and rear delts) | 8-12 | 10-14 | 14-20 | 3-5 / 5-8 | 2 / 2-3 | Presses give ~0.5 per set to front delts; rows ~0.5 to rear delts |
| Triceps | 8-12 | 10-14 | 14-20 | 3-5 / 5-8 | 2 / 2-3 | Each pressing set counts ~0.5; direct sets needed are roughly half the total |
| Biceps | 8-12 | 10-14 | 14-20 | 3-5 / 5-8 | 2 / 2-3 | Each row or pulldown set counts ~0.5 |
| Calves, abs | 8-12 | 10-14 | 14-20 | 3-5 / 5-8 | 2 / 2-3 | Little indirect credit; usually trained at the low end |

Arithmetic note: in a 3-day week with two exposures per muscle, hitting 8-12 weekly sets needs 4-5 sets per exposure; with three exposures (a full-body rotation), 3-4 sets per exposure is enough. Both stay inside the 3-5 sets-per-session band.

Per-session ceiling: **~8-10 hard sets per muscle per session** (section 2). More days buy more weekly sets; they do not make each set more effective.

### Evidence: strength and weekly volume

| Study | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Ralston et al. 2017 (Sports Med; 9 studies) | High vs low weekly sets: combined-exercise ES 0.18 (95% CI 0.06-0.30, p = 0.003); isolation ES 0.23 (0.06-0.40). Not enough data to say 10-12 sets beat 5-9. [PMC5684266](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5684266/) | Moderate |
| Pelland et al. 2025/26 | Direct-set counting fits strength best; "diminishing returns for strength being considerably more pronounced" than for hypertrophy. A weekly PUOS of ~3 fractional sets is **(secondary, unverified)**. | High for direction |
| Androulakis-Korakakis et al. 2020 (6 studies, trained men) and 2021 (powerlifters) | A single set of 6-12 reps at 70-85% 1RM, 2-3x/week to failure, gave "suboptimal, yet significant" 1RM gains; ~3-6 heavy sets of 1-5 reps per week at >80% 1RM maintained or improved 1RM over 6-12 weeks. [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2021.713655/epub) | Moderate |
| Schoenfeld 2019 MSSE (above) | 1 set = 5 sets for strength gains in trained men. | Moderate |
| Enes et al. 2024 (MSSE; trained males, 12 wk) | Adding 4-6 quad sets every 2 weeks (22 up to ~52/wk) improved lower-limb strength more than constant volume; hypertrophy "interpret with caution." [BarBend](https://barbend.com/news/you-dont-need-more-training-volume/) | Low-moderate; single trial |

**What this means for the program.** Volume is the main driver of hypertrophy up to roughly 20 sets, and a cheap input for strength. The 5-6-day weeks exist to let weekly sets rise toward 14-20 per muscle without any session exceeding the per-session ceiling. The 3-day weeks run 8-12 sets and lose little (section 7).

### Renaissance Periodization landmarks (coaching convention, not evidence)

RP's MV / MEV / MAV / MRV framework (maintenance, minimum effective, maximum adaptive, maximum recoverable volume) brackets the research range: the only official per-muscle example is chest, at MV ~4-6, MEV ~6, MRV ~20 sets at 2 sessions and ~25 at 3 ([RP chest guide](https://rpstrength.com/chest-training-tips-hypertrophy/)). Every other per-muscle table circulating online is a third-party reconstruction and the tables disagree with each other (FVI Q3). Treat landmark numbers as starting points with roughly +/-5 sets of uncertainty, and note that Pelland's "~4 fractional sets exceed the smallest detectable effect" sits below most quoted MEVs, while Räntilä 2026 and Aube 2022 show no benefit near typical MRVs.

## 2. The per-session set ceiling

### Rules

1. Plan **no more than ~8-10 hard sets per muscle per session**. For direct hard sets on a heavy compound, **6-8** is the safer planning cap.
2. If weekly volume will not fit under that ceiling in the sessions available, cut weekly volume; do not stack more sets into a session.
3. For strength, the first **~2 heavy direct sets per lift per session** do most of the work; additional heavy sets are for practice and volume, not for more 1RM.

### Evidence

| Source | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Remmert, Pelland, Robinson, Hinson & Zourdos 2025 ([SportRxiv 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537); 67 studies) | Per-session volume slope positive for both outcomes, but the point beyond which more sets no longer have a >50% chance of exceeding the smallest detectable effect was **~11 fractional sets/session for hypertrophy and ~2 direct sets/session for strength**. Layne Norton: "They didn't really find a cap," i.e. the slope stays positive but undetectably small. | Preprint, not peer reviewed; the 11 / 2 figures are from [Biolayne REPS 36](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-36/too-much-muscle-math-finding-the-ideal-training-volume-per-session/) **(secondary, unverified)**; the FAU release confirms the framing |
| Krieger 2009-2010 meta-analyses **(older)** | Set dose-response per exercise plateaus around 4-6 sets per session. [PMC5969184](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5969184) | Older, foundational |
| Henselmans, citing an informal Krieger analysis | Rule of thumb of no more than ~6 sets per muscle per session (FFP KQ3). [Henselmans](https://mennohenselmans.com/this-is-the-max-volume-you-should-do-per-workout/) | Coach opinion; conflict with ~11 largely explained by direct vs fractional counting |
| RP chest example | MRV ~20 sets/week at 2 sessions rising to ~25 at 3, which implicitly encodes a ~8-10 set per-session ceiling. | Coaching convention |
| "10 sets per muscle per session" cap | **No primary source found**; it appears only in secondary blogs. | Flagged as unsourced in FVI Q2 |

**What this means for the program.** The ceiling is the mechanism that makes frequency matter: 16 weekly sets fit in two sessions of 8, not one session of 16. It is also why a 3-day week cannot simply carry a 6-day week's volume: 18 quad sets in one session exceeds Remmert's threshold by ~60% and the extra sets are near-zero return at a real recovery cost (FVI Q7).

## 3. Frequency: how often to train each muscle

### Rules

1. **Every muscle at least 2x per week** in every week type. 2-3x in 5-6-day weeks.
2. Treat frequency as a **volume-distribution tool**. Pick weekly sets first, then spread them so no session exceeds the ceiling.
3. **Main lifts 2x per week even in a 3-day week**, because strength has a modest independent frequency benefit (skill practice, higher per-set quality).
4. Below ~10 weekly sets per muscle, once per week is probably fine (Stronger By Science reading); above it, 2x+ is needed to stay under the ceiling.

### Evidence: hypertrophy

| Study | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016 (Sports Med; 10 studies) **(older)** | Higher frequency beat lower on a binary split (ES 0.49 +/- 0.08 vs 0.30 +/- 0.07, p = 0.002); 2x > 1x; whether 3x > 2x "remains to be determined." Driven by non-volume-equated studies. [PubMed 27102172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/) | Superseded |
| Schoenfeld, Grgic & Krieger 2019 (J Sports Sci 37(11); 25 studies, 836 participants) | Frequency "does not significantly or meaningfully impact muscle hypertrophy when volume is equated." The effect appeared only when volume was not equated, and even then 1 vs 3+ days was "modest." [SBS summary](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/frequency-muscle/) | High |
| Pelland et al. 2025/26 | With volume in the model, +0.32% per extra training day (95% CrI -0.14 to 0.82%), "compatible with negligible effects." | High |
| Hamarsland et al. 2022 (Front Physiol; 21 trained completers, 9 wk) | 2 vs 4 sessions/week, equal volume: no differences in lean mass, vastus lateralis thickness, or 1RM. Introduction lists no-difference findings for 3 vs 6 (Colquhoun 2018; Saric 2019), 2 vs 4 (Johnsen & van den Tillaar 2021), 1 vs 5 (Gomes 2019); Zaroni 2019 is the lone exception. [PMC8766679](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8766679) | Moderate; small arms (n = 8-13) |
| Norwegian Frequency Project (reported by Stronger By Science) | In well-trained powerlifters, 6x/week beat 3x/week with volume equated (FFP KQ2). [SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/research-spotlight-frequency/) | Single study never published in full; best read as "frequency can improve set quality at very high volumes" |
| Stronger By Science pooled analysis | Overall frequency effect trivial (d = 0.113; CI 0.07-0.16); 0.42%/week growth low-frequency vs 0.58%/week high (`research/splits-and-named-programs.md` KQ1). | Secondary analysis |

### Evidence: strength

| Study | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Grgic et al. 2018 (Sports Med 48(5)) | Dose-response across all studies; restricted to volume-equated studies, no significant effect (p = 0.421). Higher frequency is mainly a vehicle for more volume. [VU repository](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/37695/) | High |
| Ralston et al. 2018 (Sports Med Open 4:36; 12 studies, 74 groups) | Higher vs lower frequency ES 0.09 (95% CI -0.06 to 0.24, p = 0.25); volume-equated ES 0.03 (-0.20 to 0.27). [Springer Open](https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s40798-018-0149-9) | High |
| Pelland et al. 2025/26 | Frequency had a 100% posterior probability of a positive strength effect even with volume in the model, with diminishing returns (1 to 2 helps more than 3 to 4). A slope of +3.27%/day (95% CrI 2.74-3.84%) is **(secondary, unverified)**. | High for direction |
| Colquhoun et al. 2018 (JSCR; trained men, 6 wk) | 3x vs 6x with identical weekly work: similar strength and body composition. [PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Jozo-Grgic/publication/327435932) | Moderate |
| Johnsen & van den Tillaar 2021 (PeerJ; 21 trained men) | 2 vs 4 sessions, volume matched: no strength advantage; spreading volume may lower per-session RPE. [PeerJ 10781](https://peerj.com/articles/10781) | Moderate |

**Where the two outcomes differ.** For hypertrophy, 2x vs 3x vs 6x per muscle is indistinguishable at equal weekly sets. For strength, extra exposures carry a small benefit that diminishes quickly, so 2x on each main lift is worth protecting in short weeks and 3x is a luxury for long ones. No meta-analysis isolates 2x vs 3x vs 4x+ in trained lifters with volume equated; the evidence is a handful of trials with 8-21 per arm (FVI Q1 gaps).

## 4. Intensity: load and rep ranges

### Rules

1. **Main-lift strength work: 3-6 reps at ~75-90% 1RM.** The research range is 1-6 reps at >=80% (FVI Q4); the program uses 3-6 so that RPE can be judged and volume accumulated.
2. **Hypertrophy work: 6-12 reps**, up to **15-20 on isolation** (occasionally 20-30), as long as the set is hard.
3. Do not chase light-load hypertrophy on compounds; it works, but it costs more fatigue and more reps for the same stimulus.

### Evidence

| Study | Hypertrophy | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 (JSCR; 21 studies; <=60% vs >60% 1RM, all to failure) | Similar between conditions | 1RM significantly greater with high load; isometric strength no difference. [PubMed 28834797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/) |
| Lopez et al. 2021 (MSSE 53(6); network meta-analysis, 28 studies, 747 adults; low >15RM, moderate 9-15RM, high <=8RM) | No difference between loads (P = .113-.469) | High and moderate > low; high vs moderate not significant (SMD 0.26-0.28, P = .068). [JYX](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/75929) |
| Schoenfeld, Grgic, Van Every & Plotkin 2021 (Sports 9(2):32) | "Muscular adaptations can be obtained, and in some cases optimized, across a wide spectrum of loading zones." The heavy/moderate/light "repetition continuum" is too rigid. [VU open access](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/42418/) | 1RM strength remains load-specific |

Confidence: high that hypertrophy is load-independent across ~30-85% 1RM when sets are hard; high that 1RM strength needs heavy practice; the high-vs-moderate strength gap is small and not statistically secure.

## 5. Proximity to failure (RPE and RIR)

### Rules

1. **Working sets at 0-3 reps in reserve.** Default on compounds: **1-2 RIR (RPE 8-9)**.
2. Strength top sets: RPE 7-9, rarely to failure. Failure adds fatigue without a strength benefit.
3. Hypertrophy work: the last 1-2 sets of an isolation exercise may go to failure; compounds stay at 1-3 RIR to protect volume and recovery.
4. Use the RIR-based RPE scale (RPE 10 = 0 RIR, 9 = 1 RIR, 8 = 2 RIR). Expect your estimate to be accurate within ~1 rep only when you are 0-3 reps from failure; "RPE 7" on a compound is noisy. Anchor most hypertrophy sets at RPE 8-9 because that is where the estimate is reliable.

### Evidence

| Study | Hypertrophy | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Robinson, Pelland, Remmert, Refalo, Jukic, Steele & Zourdos 2024 (Sports Med 54(9); meta-regression on estimated RIR) | "Seems to improve upon terminating sets closer to failure"; modest, possibly non-linear | In all best-fit models "the confidence intervals of the marginal slopes for estimated RIR contained a null point estimate." RIR values were estimated from study descriptions; exact slopes not retrievable. [Abertay repository](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-the-dose-response-relationship-between-estimated-resist/) |
| Grgic et al. 2022 (J Sport Health Sci; 15 studies) | Failure vs non-failure ES 0.22 (95% CI -0.11 to 0.55), NS; 2-study trained subgroup tentatively favoured failure | ES -0.09 (95% CI -0.22 to 0.05), NS. [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/dc36ba7ccd994798964f66b93bd8f662) |
| Refalo et al. 2023 | No meaningful difference (Biolayne summary); a small edge for closer-to-failure, ES 0.15-0.21, is **(secondary, unverified)** | n/a |
| Strength meta-analysis on failure, likely Carvalho et al. 2022 (authorship not confirmed) | n/a | Small pooled effect favouring **non**-failure (ES 0.34, p = 0.02); NS in volume-controlled studies, trend still favouring non-failure. [sport-iat record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4039453) |
| Zourdos, Helms et al. 2016 (JSCR 30:267-275) | RIR-based RPE scale; strong inverse relationship between bar velocity and RPE; traditional Borg RPE only reaches 6.8-9.0 at failure. [AUT repository](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/efef3b25-6701-4fb5-bb82-55fcd2a26027/full) | Same |
| Helms 2018 (Front Physiol) and Russo et al. 2025 (systematic review) | RIR accuracy is better close to failure and at higher loads; it degrades as reps rise and %1RM falls. [Helms 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2018.00247/pdf) | Same |

Confidence: moderate. No high-quality trial compares RPE-anchored and %1RM-anchored programming head-to-head for hypertrophy in intermediates (FVI Q4 gaps).

## 6. Rest intervals

### Rules

1. **2-3 minutes between sets of compounds.**
2. **60-90 seconds between sets of isolation and machine work.** Going under 60 s costs reps and therefore effective volume.
3. On time-constrained days, antagonist supersets with 60-90 s between movements preserve the >60 s per-muscle rest while cutting session time. This is an inference, not a tested protocol (FVI Q5).

### Evidence

| Study | Finding | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Singer, Wolf, Schoenfeld et al. 2024 ("Give it a rest," Front Sports Act Living; Bayesian meta-analysis) | "Small hypertrophic benefit to employing inter-set rest interval durations >60 s, perhaps mediated by reductions in volume load"; no appreciable difference when resting >90 s; whole-body estimate near zero. Reported SMDs of 0.48 short vs 0.56 long are **(secondary, unverified)**. No strength outcomes in the abstract. [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/text) | Hypertrophy |
| Grgic, Lazinica, Mikulic, Krieger & Schoenfeld 2017 (Eur J Sport Sci; systematic review) | Both short and long rest viable for untrained; "a possible advantage for the use of long rest intervals" in trained participants. [VU repository](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/44281/) | Hypertrophy |
| Schoenfeld et al. 2016 (JSCR 30:1805-1812; 21 trained men, 8 wk, 1 vs 3 min) | 3-min group gained more 1RM squat and bench; anterior-thigh thickness significantly greater, trends elsewhere. The viral "93% more growth" figure is undocumented. [NASE summary](https://www.naseinc.com/blog/how-long-should-you-rest-between-sets-for-size-and-strength/) | Strength and hypertrophy |

Confidence: moderate for hypertrophy (one Bayesian meta-analysis); low-moderate for strength (one trained-subject trial plus mechanism). No meta-analysis quantifies rest effects on strength specifically.

## 7. The maintenance dose and what it means for short weeks

### Rules

1. **Intensity (load) is the variable to protect.** Every maintenance finding is conditional on relative load being kept.
2. A week at **one-third to one-half of peak weekly volume at normal loads maintains and usually still builds** for a lifter under about 40. A 3-day week at 8-12 sets per muscle is a slow-progress week, not a lost week.
3. When cutting, keep the main-lift top sets heavy (RPE 8-9) and cut accessory and back-off sets first.
4. **Age flag:** lifters in their late 40s and 50s should plan short weeks at the one-half end of the range and keep two sessions per muscle. The older groups in the maintenance studies needed more.
5. A single bad week (<=7 days off) costs nothing measurable; return to the rotation without make-up sessions.

### Evidence

| Study | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Spiering, Mujika, Sharp & Foulis 2021 (JSCR 35(5):1449-1458; narrative review) | Muscle size in younger people maintained up to 32 weeks with as little as **1 session/week and 1 set/exercise**, "as long as exercise intensity (relative load) is maintained"; older people likely need up to 2 sessions/week and 2-3 sets/exercise; "intensity appears to be the key variable"; insufficient data for athletes. [PubMed 33629972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33629972/) | Moderate (review) |
| Bickel, Cross & Bamman 2011 (MSSE; 70 adults) **(older)** | 16 weeks of 3x/week training, then 32 weeks at detraining, 1/3 dose, or 1/9 dose. Young (20-35): both 1/3 and 1/9 preserved myofiber hypertrophy and the **1/3 dose produced additional hypertrophy**. Older (60-75): neither reduced dose preserved hypertrophy. Strength was largely retained even through full detraining, with slight loss at 32 weeks. Exact retention percentages not retrievable. [PubMed 21131862](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131862/) | Moderate; subjects were untrained before the 16-week run-in |
| Androulakis-Korakakis 2020/2021 (trained men; powerlifters) | 1 set of 6-12 at 70-85% 1RM 2-3x/week, or ~3-6 heavy sets of 1-5 per week at >80%, still increased 1RM over 6-12 weeks. | Moderate |
| RP maintenance volume (chest) | ~4-6 sets/week for experienced trainers, consistent with the maintenance literature. [RP help](https://help.rpstrength.com/hc/en-us/articles/32433153518359-Chest) | Coaching convention |
| Detraining timelines (FFP KQ3) | One week off: no measurable size loss, 3-7% strength dip that returns in 1-2 sessions; noticeable loss after ~3-4 weeks of complete inactivity; a 4-week layoff cut 1RM ~6-9% in trained men. [Nippard](https://jeffnippard.com/blogs/news/how-to-get-your-gains-back-after-a-training-break); [SetGraph](https://setgraph.app/articles/does-a-week-off-the-gym-kill-your-gains) | Secondary summaries |

### What it means for the 3-day week

- A 12-18-set week cut to 4-6 sets per muscle is already at Bickel's 1/3 dose, which built additional muscle in young adults. The program's 3-day target of 8-12 sets sits comfortably above that floor.
- The 3-day week should **not** be built by compressing 6-day volume into fewer sessions (section 2). It should run lower total volume at the same loads.
- The volume-equated frequency trials (Colquhoun 3 vs 6; Hamarsland 2 vs 4) say that where sets *are* equal, the number of sessions does not matter. The maintenance trials say that where sets are *not* equal, keeping load protects what you have. Together they justify holding intensity constant across week types and letting accessory volume float.
- Caveats: no study has tested alternating high- and low-frequency weeks within one program, and no maintenance study covers lifters with more than 16 weeks of prior training (FVI Q6-Q7 gaps). The recommendation is assembled from steady-state comparisons.

How the sessions are laid out, what to cut first, and how to resume a rotation after a gap are owned by `guide/03-flexible-scheduling.md` and `templates/`.

## 8. Strength versus hypertrophy: one-table summary

| Variable | For 1RM strength on the main lifts | For muscle size |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly volume | Saturates early: 2-6 heavy direct sets per lift per session, 2-3x/week. Schoenfeld 2019: 1 set = 5 sets. | Positive to ~20 sets/week with diminishing returns (Schoenfeld 2017; Baz-Valle 2022; Pelland 2025/26). |
| Per-session ceiling | ~2 direct sets before returns become undetectable (Remmert 2025, secondary). | ~11 fractional sets (Remmert 2025, secondary); plan at 8-10. |
| Frequency | Small independent benefit, diminishing (Pelland); 2x per lift worth protecting. | None detectable at equal volume (Schoenfeld 2019; Hamarsland 2022). |
| Load | Specific: heavy (>=~75-80%) beats light (Lopez 2021; Schoenfeld 2017). | Load-independent ~30-85% when sets are hard. |
| Proximity to failure | No relationship with RIR (Robinson 2024); failure slightly worse when volume uncontrolled. | Modest benefit from closer to failure (Robinson 2024). |
| Rest | 3 min > 1 min in trained men (Schoenfeld 2016). | >60 s helps; nothing extra past ~90 s (Singer 2024). |
| Maintenance | Retained through months of detraining (Bickel 2011); keep load. | 1 session, 1 set/exercise per week at full load holds size in young adults (Spiering 2021). |
