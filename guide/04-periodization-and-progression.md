# 04. Periodization and progression

Last updated: 2026-10-21

This chapter covers how training is organised over weeks and months: periodization models and what the evidence says about them, the progression schemes used by named programs with their exact rules, deloads, mesocycle structure, plateaus, training age, and what to log. It explains the options and the evidence. **The rules this program actually uses for each main lift are in `templates/progression-tracks.md`; this chapter does not restate them.** How a mesocycle is counted when the week length varies is owned by `guide/03-flexible-scheduling.md`.

Source note: evidence comes from `research/periodization-and-progression.md` (PP, Q1-Q7) and `research/flexible-frequency-programming.md` (FFP, KQ4, KQ6). The research note tags each finding **[Evidence]** (peer-reviewed) or **[Convention]** (program author or coaching practice); those tags are kept here. Figures the note could not confirm from a primary text are marked **(unverified)**.

## 1. Periodization models

### What the terms mean

| Model | Definition | Typical structure (convention) |
|---|---|---|
| Non-periodized | Same sets, reps, and relative load throughout; only the weight changes. | Linear progression programs are "non-periodized" in the research sense. |
| Linear (LP) | Reps fall and intensity rises across a block. | 4-6 weeks from ~65-75% toward 85-95%. |
| Undulating (DUP daily / WUP weekly) | Reps and intensity vary session to session or week to week. | Heavy / medium / light days; or a 10s week, 5s week, 3s week. |
| Block (Issurin-style) | Accumulation (longest), transmutation/intensification, realization (shortest). | Secondary sources: accumulation 2-6 weeks, transmutation up to ~4, realization 1-2; one powerlifting example 5 / 4 / 3; a Bondarchuk-method summary wants transmutation ~3 and realization at most 2. Sources disagree. |
| Conjugate (Westside) | Max-effort lifts rotate every 1-3 weeks; dynamic-effort work at ~50-70% 1RM for speed in 3-week waves. | Critics argue the constant variation costs specificity for raw lifters. |

### Evidence

**Periodized vs non-periodized**

| Study | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| [Evidence] Williams et al. 2017 (Sports Med; 81 effects, 18 studies 1988-2015) | 1RM gains larger with periodization, ES = 0.43 (95% CI 0.27-0.58). Undulating more favourable than linear; training status and frequency moderated the effect. [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/Comparison-of-Periodized-and-Non-Periodized-Resistance.pdf) | Moderate |
| [Evidence/Analysis] Stronger By Science re-analysis of Williams 2017 | After adjusting for nested effects and funnel-plot asymmetry, d = 0.23 +/- 0.05, still significant. Confound: periodized groups reach higher peak loads, so the effect may be peak intensity rather than sequencing. The only pair of studies matching volume, average, and peak intensity found no advantage. [SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=52473) | Moderate |
| [Evidence] Moesgaard et al. 2022 (Sports Med; 35 studies) | 1RM ES = 0.31 (95% CI 0.04-0.57) favouring periodization; the advantage held **only in trained** people, not in the previously untrained; **no greater hypertrophy**. One summary's description of the sample mix sits awkwardly with the trained/untrained framing; verify in the full paper. [PubMed 35044672](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/) | Moderate-high |

**Linear vs undulating**

| Study | Finding | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| [Evidence] Harries, Lubans & Callister 2015 (JSCR 29(4); 17 studies, 510 participants) | No difference between linear and undulating for upper- or lower-body strength; short durations and prior training flagged as confounders. [PubMed 25268290](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25268290/) | Strength |
| [Evidence] Grgic et al. 2017 (PeerJ; 13 studies) | LP vs DUP hypertrophy: d = -0.02 (95% CI -0.25 to 0.21). No difference. [PeerJ 3695](https://peerj.com/articles/3695) | Hypertrophy |
| [Evidence] 2016 meta-analysis, 25 studies | Undulating beat traditional for 1RM, ES = 0.22; hypertrophy ES 0.32 (CI -0.07 to 0.71), not significant. [Record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4043930) | Both |

**Block vs DUP in trained populations**

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| [Evidence] Gavanda et al. 2019 (47 adolescent football players, 12 wk) | Both improved mass, strength, jump, sprint; no between-group difference. |
| [Evidence] Painter et al. 2012 (31 D-I track athletes, 10 wk) | Trends favoured block for strength and RFD, not significant; block had better gains per unit of volume (a derived efficiency metric). |
| [Evidence] Sabido et al. 2018 (15 handball players, 8 wk, matched) | Both improved squat and triple jump; DUP better for throwing velocity. Very small sample. |

### What it means

- For a trained lifter, **some** periodization beats none for 1RM by a small margin (d ~0.2-0.3). It makes **no detectable difference for hypertrophy**. The mechanism is probably reaching heavier peak loads and managing fatigue, not the sequence itself.
- Linear vs undulating vs block is preference and schedule. Daily undulation (heavy / medium / light exposures) suits a lifter who touches each lift often in a short week; block suits a lifter peaking for a date. Conjugate's exercise rotation is a poor match for an intermediate who needs practice on the competition lifts (coaching opinion, consistent with specificity).
- Gaps: no meta-analysis pools block vs DUP; the newer SBS periodization synthesis and Issurin's own block durations were not accessible (PP Q1 gaps).

## 2. Progression schemes with exact rules

The only progression comparisons with controlled evidence are RPE vs percentage loading and velocity-loss thresholds (section 2.8). Everything else below is **[Convention]**: program-author rules that work in practice but have not been tested against each other. The operating rule is: pick one scheme per lift, apply its stall rule literally, and change schemes only after the stall rule has fired.

### 2.1 Linear progression (add load every session)

| Program | Increment | Stall trigger | Reset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting Strength | +5 lb squat, bench, press; +10 lb deadlift while it lasts | Fail all reps at the same weight 3 sessions in a row | Drop 5-10% ("The Reset: Why and How"), rebuild | 
| StrongLifts 5x5 | +2.5 kg (5 lb) per workout; deadlift +5 kg or +10 lb by source | 3 failed workouts at a weight | -10% on that lift only |
| Greyskull LP | +2.5 lb upper, +5 lb lower per session; top set 1x5+ | Fewer than 5 reps on the top set | -10% next session |
| r/Fitness BBR | +2.5 lb upper, +5 lb lower per session; larger jumps if the AMRAP exceeds 10 | Fewer than 15 total reps across the sets | -10% |
| Kg beginner guide | Bench and press 1.25-2.5 kg per session | | |

Stronger By Science's novice fix: when a rep scheme stalls, deload 10-15% and rebuild through progressively lower reps with more sets (4x8 to 6x5 to 6x3); if the 8-rep day stalls before the 5- or 3-rep day, drop ~15%, add a set to the stalled lift, and resume. [SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/making-your-novice-strength-training-routine-more-effective-two-quick-tips/)

Use linear progression only while it works. Switch to weekly or cycle-based progression when per-session jumps fail twice in a row on a lift (PP Q2 inference).

### 2.2 Double progression (reps, then load)

- **Standard:** fix a load. Add reps each session until every working set hits the top of the range (for example 8-12) with good form. Then increase load and drop back to the bottom of the range. Start with a load you can lift for at least the bottom of the range with a couple of reps in reserve.
- **Dynamic (Helms, Muscle & Strength Pyramid):** each set is independent; increase load on a specific set next week only if that set exceeded the range.
- Best fit: accessories and hypertrophy work at 8-15 reps. This is the program's default for accessories (design spine item 5).

### 2.3 Triple progression (reps, then sets, then load)

- Add reps to the top of the range; then add a set; then add load and drop sets and reps back. The Pyramid variant requires exceeding the rep range by 3-4 quality reps before adding load.
- Best fit: 5-6-day lifters with room to add sets. In this program, the extra set is the first thing a short week removes, so triple progression is only sensible on accessories a lifter trains in every week type.

### 2.4 Wave and percentage schemes

**5/3/1 (Wendler)** [Convention]

- Training max (TM): 90% of true 1RM in the original book; 85% in the later "hardgainers" guidance; stronger lifters do better with a lower TM. Check: if you cannot get at least 5 reps at 95% of TM, the TM is too high.
- Weeks: 65/75/85% x 5/5/5+; 70/80/90% x 3/3/3+; 75/85/95% x 5/3/1+; deload 40/50/60% x 5. "+" is an AMRAP, not to grinding failure.
- Progression: after each cycle, +5 lb to bench and press TM, +10 lb to squat and deadlift TM. The increase is to the TM, not the 1RM. "If any of your reps fail to adhere to these standards during your training cycle, YOU DO NOT INCREASE YOUR TRAINING MAX." No re-max needed.
- Stall: when AMRAPs stop beating prior cycles, reset that lift's TM to ~90% of its current value and rebuild.
- *Forever*: leader blocks (two 3-week waves of volume work) then anchor blocks (one 3-week wave of intensity work); a TM test replaces the deload: 70/80/90% x 5 then 100% of TM for 3-5 reps; miss and you lower the TM. (Reader-transcribed; Wendler's "5 forward, 3 back" rule and the exact test scheme were **not confirmed** from his own text.) [JimWendler.com hardgainers](https://www.jimwendler.com/blogs/jimwendler-com/5-3-1-for-hardgainers)

**Juggernaut Method (Smith)** [Convention]: four 4-week waves (10s, 8s, 5s, 3s), each accumulation, intensification, realization, deload; reps beaten on the realization AMRAP bump the TM; exact formula **(unverified)**.

**Texas Method (weekly percentage progression)** [Convention]: Monday volume 5x5 at ~90% of Friday's 5RM (about 10% below), Wednesday light (~80% of Monday; sources disagree), Friday a new 1x5 PR. ~5 lb per week on squat and deadlift; bench and press about every two weeks because they alternate. When Friday stalls, reduce Monday volume rather than forcing the load.

**Hybrid for this program:** percentage targets with an RPE cap, or back-offs set as a percentage of the day's estimated 1RM, capture most of both approaches (PP Q2 inference). The chosen form is in `templates/progression-tracks.md`.

### 2.5 Rep-max and rep-scheme ladders

**GZCL / GZCLP (Lefever)** [Convention]

- Tiers: T1 main compound 85-100% of TM, ~10-15 total reps per workout (1-3 per set); T2 65-85%, 20-30 total reps (5-8 per set); T3 accessories at or under 65%, 30+ total reps (GZCLP table: 45+); about 1:2:3 T1:T2:T3 reps across the week. "Soft" gradients.
- GZCLP T1 ladder: 5x3+ at a load, +5 lb upper / +10 lb lower per completed session; when 15 base reps are not achieved, keep the weight and move to 6x2+, then 10x1+; after 10x1+ fails, test a new 5RM and restart at ~85% (mirrors conflict on whether the TM is a 2RM or 85% of a 5RM; the 5RM/85% version matches the reset rule). T2 ladder: 3x10, 3x8, 3x6; after 3x6 fails, back to 3x10 at slightly more than the last 3x10 weight. T3: add load when the AMRAP set reaches 25 reps.

**nSuns** [Convention]: progression keyed to reps on the T1 "1+" set each week: 0-1 reps no increase; 2-3 reps +5 lb; more than 5 reps +10-15 lb; T2 +5 lb upper / +10 lb lower; stuck at 1 rep or failing, reduce the training 1RM by 10%.

### 2.6 RPE / RIR-based loading

- **Scale:** RPE 10 = 0 reps in reserve, 9 = 1 RIR, 8 = 2 RIR (Zourdos, Helms et al. 2016; `guide/01-training-principles.md`, section 5).
- **Load selection:** choose the load that produces the target RPE for the prescribed reps today. Percentages become guidance, not prescription.
- **Reactive Training Systems fatigue percents (Tuchscherer)** [Convention]: work up to a top set at RPE 9-10, drop the load by a fatigue percentage, and repeat sets until the reduced load also reaches the target RPE. Example: 300x3 @9, then sets at 285x3 until that is a 9 = 5% fatigue. Tiers: low ~3% or less, medium ~5%, high ~7% or more. The RPE-to-%1RM table values were not retrieved **(unverified)**.
- **Barbell Medicine e1RM method** [Convention]: compute an estimated 1RM each workout from the most accurate set available (5 @ RPE 9 or a single @ RPE 8) and set back-off loads as a percentage of today's e1RM instead of a stale tested max. Overshooting RPE "is not a reason for great concern"; finish the set and adjust later sets. "Continuing to blindly add weight to the bar despite it clearly being unsustainable is not a smart way to train." [BBM forum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/e1rm-calculation-in-the-templates/664)
- **RPE stops for volume** [Evidence/Convention]: "do back-off sets until a set reaches RPE 9, maximum N sets." A thesis on 12 powerlifters found volume "can be effectively autoregulated using RPE stops" (FFP KQ4). This makes a session shrink automatically on a compressed or tired day.

### 2.7 Velocity-based training

- Rules from the evidence: a velocity-loss cut-off of **~20-25% or less** within a set favours strength and power; **more than 25%** favours hypertrophy at a fatigue cost; 30-40% reliably raises fatigue and recovery demand; 10-20% preserves velocity.
- Without a device, approximate with RIR: stopping at 2-4 RIR corresponds to the low-velocity-loss region; grinding to 0-1 RIR corresponds to high velocity loss (PP Q2 inference).

### 2.8 Evidence comparing progression and loading methods

| Study | Comparison | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Evidence] Helms et al. 2018 (Front Physiol; 21 trained men, 8 wk DUP, squat and bench 3x/wk) | RPE-chosen loads vs %1RM | Squat +17.05 kg (RPE) vs +13.91 kg (%); bench +10.70 vs +9.64 kg; not statistically significant; magnitude-based inference gave RPE a small edge. [PMC5877330](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5877330/) | Low-moderate (n = 21) |
| [Evidence] Hickmott et al. 2022 (Sports Med Open; 15 studies, trained) | Autoregulated load (RPE or velocity) vs percentage | 1RM MD = 2.07 kg (95% CI -0.32 to 4.46, p = 0.09, SMD 0.21), not significant. No study tested chronic effects of RPE-stop volume. [PMC8762534](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8762534/) | Moderate |
| [Evidence] Hickmott 2022 | Velocity loss <=25% vs >25% | 1RM advantage MD 2.32 kg (95% CI 0.33-4.31, p = 0.02, SMD 0.23) for <=25%; smaller CSA gains (MD 0.61 cm2, SMD 0.28). | Moderate |
| [Evidence] Zhang et al. 2023 (dose-response meta-analysis) | Velocity-loss thresholds | 20-30% loss may be best for maximal strength; lower loss more efficient for maintaining strength. [PMC9914552](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9914552/) | Moderate |
| [Evidence] Jukic et al. 2023 (Sports Med) | Velocity-loss thresholds | Secondhand: strength equivalent at 10% vs 40% loss; each 10% more loss slightly more hypertrophy (beta 0.006) **(unverified)**. | Low until verified |
| [Evidence] Pareja-Blanco (8-wk squat) | 20% vs 40% velocity loss | Similar squat strength; 20% group improved jumps more with fewer reps. | Single trial |
| [Evidence] Weakley et al. 2020 | 10 / 20 / 30% thresholds | Reliably different rep counts and kinetic outputs (CV <10%). | Methodological |
| [Evidence] Helms et al. 2020 review (J Hum Kinet; 90 studies) | Autoregulation methods | Performance-based load progression, flexible configurations, RIR-based RPE and velocity adjustments are "practical, reliable and show preliminary utility." Borg RPE under-reports effort (6.8-9.0 at failure). [JHK](https://johk.pl/?p=5643) | Review |

Bottom line: RPE-based loading is at least as good as percentages and more robust to daily variation; percentages are easier to execute while learning RPE. No controlled trial compares linear vs double vs triple progression (PP Q2 gaps).

## 3. Deloads

### Rules (design spine item 5)

1. Deload **roughly every 4-6 weeks, or sooner when readiness indicators say so**.
2. **Keep frequency. Halve the sets. Keep some heavy-but-easy work** (top sets around RPE 6-7). Do not take a full week off mid-block if strength is a goal.
3. Reactive triggers that justify an early deload: missed reps at the prescribed RPE in two consecutive sessions on a lift; rising RPE at the same load across the block; soreness that does not clear between exposures; disrupted sleep or appetite; joint pain.
4. A deload can double as the first, low-volume week of the next mesocycle (75% Delphi agreement).

### Evidence

| Source | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| [Evidence] Coleman et al. 2024 (PeerJ; 39 trained participants, 9-wk high-volume program) | One week of **complete** rest at the midpoint vs continuous training: continuous training showed greater isometric and dynamic lower-body strength gains; no appreciable differences in lower-body size, local endurance, or power; continuous had slightly better readiness-to-train. Authors: a 1-week deload at the midpoint "appears to negatively influence lower-body strength but not hypertrophy, power or endurance." [PeerJ 16777](https://peerj.com/articles/16777) | Only RCT; tested cessation, **not** a volume-reduced deload, so it cannot be extrapolated to practical deloads. A Biolayne write-up says 8 weeks; the paper says 9. Trust the paper. |
| [Evidence] 8-week study in beginners | No statistical disadvantage for a deload condition vs continuous training in muscle thickness or 10RM. [Sci-Sport](https://sci-sport.com/en/active-deload-in-strength-training-does-temporarily-reducing-volume-hinder-progress-270/) | Beginners only |
| [Convention/Qualitative] Bell et al. 2022 (Front Sports Act Living; interviews with 18 weightlifting, powerlifting, bodybuilding coaches) | No single method; volume and intensity of effort were the most-adjusted variables; typical duration 5-7 days; programmed every 4-6 weeks though periodicity varied widely. [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.1073223/full) | Expert practice |
| [Convention/Consensus] Bell et al. 2023 Delphi (Sports Med Open 9:87; 34/29/21 coaches over three rounds) | Definition: "a period of reduced training stress designed to mitigate physiological and psychological fatigue, promote recovery, and enhance preparedness for subsequent training." Volume, effort, and load are the usual targets; typical duration one week; **frequency usually maintained**; may be coach-, athlete-, or data-driven; 75% agreed a deload can sit in the first week of a new mesocycle; two camps on proactive vs reactive, with a later paper noting growing preference for flexible timing. Exact percentage reductions not retrieved. [PMC10511399](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10511399/) | Expert consensus |
| [Convention] 5/3/1 | Week 4 at 40/50/60% of TM x 5: both intensity and volume cut. | Program rule |
| [Convention] RP (third-party summaries) | One week at ~50% volume with reduced intensity after each block; 4:1 to 6:1 training weeks per deload week. | Program rule |
| [Convention] Schoenfeld | The need for deloads "is going to vary substantially between individuals." | Opinion |

### Reconciling Coleman 2024 with the coaches

Stopping entirely cost strength; coaches never stop entirely. Keeping frequency and some heavy work while halving sets cuts fatigue without letting the strength expression detrain. Cutting volume rather than intensity also matches the maintenance evidence that load is the variable to protect (`guide/01-training-principles.md`, section 7). No study compares volume-only, intensity-only, and full-rest deloads head-to-head in trained lifters, and the Pritchard 2015 taper review was not reachable, so taper percentages are not given here (PP Q3 gaps).

For an intermediate whose fatigue tracks life stress, reactive deloading fits better than a fixed calendar; the 4-6-week proactive default is for someone who will not monitor closely.

## 4. Mesocycle structure

### The hypertrophy block (RP-style volume ramp) [Convention]

- Start near minimum effective volume, add about **one set per muscle per week** over **4-6 weeks** until performance stalls or volume nears the recoverable ceiling, then deload about one week and restart lower.
- Signals to end a block: near-zero reps in reserve at prior loads; volume approaching the ceiling; soreness, pump, performance, and joint feedback trending the wrong way.
- Landmark numbers conflict across sources. RP's own chest guide gives intermediate MEV 4-6, MAV 6-16, MRV 16-24 weekly sets; an unofficial compilation lists chest MV 8 / MEV 10 / MAV 12-20 / MRV 22+, quads MEV 8, back MEV 10 / MRV 25+; a Boxrox summary gives chest 12-20. Treat them as starting hypotheses. The often-attributed 3-to-1 RIR ramp across a block was **not confirmed** in any retrieved source.
- A practical illustration (PP Q4 inference, not a prescription): week 1 at roughly 8-10 hard sets per muscle at 3-4 RIR; add 1-2 sets per muscle per week and tighten to 1-2 RIR by week 4-5; deload in week 5 or 6 at ~50% of sets with loads at ~85-90% of the prior week; restart the next block 1-2 sets above the previous week-1 volume if the block produced PRs, otherwise at the same starting volume.

### The strength block

- The volume ramp inverts: hold weekly sets flat or slightly falling while intensity climbs (65-75% toward 80-90%), matching accumulation, intensification, realization.
- 5/3/1 is a 4-week mesocycle (3 loading weeks plus deload) with the TM rising 5/10 lb per cycle. Block periodization examples run accumulation 2-6 weeks, transmutation up to 4, realization 1-2.

### Counting weeks when the week length varies

In this program a "week" of a mesocycle is a count of **exposures per lift**, not a calendar week. In the rotation one pass equals one calendar week for any 3- to 6-day week, because every main lift gets its heavy and volume exposure in every week type; only a 2-day week or a missed session stretches a pass (FFP KQ6). 5/3/1 already works this way: a 4-week cycle takes 5+ weeks at 3 days per week. The exact counting rule is in `guide/03-flexible-scheduling.md` and `templates/progression-tracks.md`.

### Robust rule

Because the landmark numbers conflict, the robust rule is procedural: start lower than you think, add sets until recovery markers (sleep, joint pain, rep performance at a fixed load) degrade, then deload. No RCT tests MEV-to-MRV ramping against flat volume, and no comparative evidence settles 4 vs 6 vs 8-week blocks (PP Q4 gaps).

## 5. Plateaus

Research on plateaus is thin. The best-supported moves are a modest load reset (5-15%) with a change in set/rep structure, a change of relative-intensity zone, or reducing volume on the heaviest day when the top set stalls. Program-hopping is contested; switch programs only after the program's own stall rule has fired at least once without restoring progress.

### Diagnostic order for a stalled lift (PP Q5 inference)

1. Check the **e1RM trend over 3-4 weeks**, not one session. Barbell Medicine: "most of what gets called a stall is impatience with the noise floor at your current strength level."
2. Check **recovery inputs**: sleep, calories, bodyweight trend.
3. Apply the **program's reset rule** (5-15% load drop and rebuild).
4. Change the **rep or intensity zone** for 4-8 weeks (a stalled 5x5 to 4x8, or to heavier triples). One cited study found alternating 12-week low-load and moderate-load blocks helped subjects avoid a plateau. [SBS plateaus](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/plateaus/)
5. Change the **exercise variation** for a block (pause squat, tempo bench). Evidence on how often to rotate variations was not retrieved.
6. Only then change the whole program. A coach heuristic: consider a change every 4-16 weeks, and only for a plateau the program caused.

Volume reduction on the heaviest day is the fix when top sets stall; adding a set is the fix when top sets feel easy but reps do not climb.

### Named stall rules in one place

| Program | Stall definition | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Starting Strength | Same weight fails 3 sessions running | Reset 5-10%, rebuild |
| StrongLifts | 3 failed workouts at a weight | -10% on that lift |
| Greyskull | Top set under 5 reps | -10% |
| SBS novice tips | Rep scheme stalls | -10-15%, rebuild 4x8 to 6x5 to 6x3; or -15% and add a set |
| Texas Method | Friday PR missed | Reduce Monday volume (5x5 to 6x4 / 8x3, or 4x5 / 3x5); cycle intensity-day rep schemes; hold volume-day weight 2-4 weeks |
| 5/3/1 | AMRAPs stop beating prior cycles; or <5 reps at 100% TM on the TM test | TM to ~90% of current, rebuild |
| GZCLP | Base reps not achieved | Next rep scheme (5x3 to 6x2 to 10x1), then retest 5RM and restart at ~85% |
| nSuns | 0-1 reps on the 1+ set repeatedly | Reduce training 1RM 10% |
| Westside | Accommodation | Rotate max-effort exercise every 1-3 weeks (critics: costs specificity) |

## 6. Training age: the Practical Programming framework and the Barbell Medicine critique

**Rippetoe and Baker** [Convention]: advancement is defined by how long the stress-recovery-adaptation cycle takes, "not a trainee's strength or athleticism." Novices adapt between sessions, so add load every session (+5 lb squat/bench/press, +10 lb deadlift). Intermediates adapt weekly, so plan weekly PRs (Texas Method: ~5 lb/week squat and deadlift; bench and press every two weeks). Advanced lifters adapt over months. Andy Baker (per forum citation): run the Texas Method 16-24 weeks before moving to heavy-light-medium. Starting Strength's own gyms warn that lifters wrongly copy novice methods into intermediate training.

**Barbell Medicine** [Critique]: "all of the adaptations run at the same rate at year one and year ten." What changes with training age is remaining ceiling, measurement noise, and the stimulus required, not biology. Prescribing load increases on a schedule tied to a stage is the error; progression should be **reactive to demonstrated performance**. The companion podcast (Feigenbaum and Baraki, May 2026) cites a 2024 Sports Medicine analysis of 17 years of IPF data with no "kink" in the strength-progress curve by training age (reported secondhand; not retrieved). [Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/novice-intermediate-advanced-strength-training/)

**Where the evidence sits:** periodization's strength advantage appears only in trained lifters (Moesgaard 2022), and training status moderated the effect in Williams 2017, which is consistent with untrained lifters progressing on almost any progressive overload.

**Operational agreement:** both camps say add load when performance shows you can, and lengthen the time between planned PR attempts as progress slows. The disagreement is whether to pre-assign cycle length by stage or let the data choose. For a self-coached intermediate, the reactive version is safer: per-session increments until two consecutive misses, then weekly, then 3-6-week cycles. Rule of thumb consistent with both: novice 1 session; early intermediate 1 week; later intermediate 3-6-week mesocycles; advanced 8-16-week blocks with a peak (PP Q6 inference). Practical Programming's own text was not accessible; definitions come from reviews (PP Q6 gaps).

## 7. What to log

Chapter 07 covers tools and apps. The minimum record, and the criteria for judging a block, are here because they are what progression schemes run on.

**Per set:** exercise, load, reps, RPE or RIR, a note on technique or pain.
**Per session:** bodyweight, sleep hours, a 1-5 readiness score.
**Per week:** total hard sets per muscle group (fractional counting, `guide/01-training-principles.md`, section 1).
**Per lift:** an estimated 1RM (e1RM) from the day's most accurate set, which is a heavy, low-rep set at RPE 8 or higher. RIR ratings are most accurate near failure and at high loads (Russo et al. 2025), so e1RMs from long sets are less trustworthy.

**Judging a block.** Decide the success criterion before the block starts: (a) e1RM on the main lifts up at matched RPE versus week 1; or (b) more reps at the same load; or (c) the same performance at lower RPE; for hypertrophy blocks, (d) measurable circumference or bodyweight change with stable or better rep performance. Because single-session RPE is noisy, average e1RM over the last three sessions of a block and compare with the first three. If none of (a)-(c) occurred after a full block plus deload, run the plateau procedure in section 5.

Other trackable signals: 5/3/1's "+" sets are rep PRs at a fixed TM percentage; RTS fatigue percents log per-session dose; RP's soreness, pump, performance, and joint feedback adjust volume, with rising RIR at the same load, non-clearing soreness, and flattening performance as deload signals; Coleman 2024's readiness questionnaires favoured the continuous group, a reminder that subjective readiness is informative. No peer-reviewed validation of e1RM-trend or "differential RPE" as a predictor of long-term progress was found, and the claim that experienced lifters rate within 0.5 RPE of true effort came from a vendor page and is not reported as fact (PP Q7 gaps).
