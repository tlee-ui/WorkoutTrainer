# Periodization Models and Progression Schemes for Resistance Training

Research notes for an intermediate lifter training 3-6 days/week. Each finding is tagged **[Evidence]** (peer-reviewed study/meta-analysis) or **[Convention]** (program author / coaching practice, not experimentally validated). Access note: full-text fetching was blocked by the session proxy, so findings come from search-result extracts of the primary papers and program-author pages; exact figures are given only where an extract stated them.

## Key Question 1: Periodization models (linear, undulating, block, conjugate) and the evidence comparing them

### Takeaway
Periodized training beats non-periodized training for 1RM strength by a small-to-moderate margin (pooled ES roughly 0.3-0.4, shrinking to about 0.23 after bias adjustment), with the advantage clearest in trained lifters and essentially absent for hypertrophy. Among periodization styles, undulating vs linear shows at most a small edge for strength (ES ~0.22 in one meta-analysis, null in another) and no difference for hypertrophy; block vs DUP trials in trained athletes find no clear winner. Model choice is therefore secondary to doing enough volume, progressing load, and managing fatigue.

### Cited Findings

**Periodized vs non-periodized**
- [Evidence] Williams et al. 2017 (Sports Medicine): 81 effects from 18 studies (1988-2015); 1RM gains were larger with periodized training, ES = 0.43 (95% CI 0.27-0.58). Undulating programs were more favorable for strength than linear; training status and frequency also moderated 1RM change. — [Williams et al. 2017 PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/Comparison-of-Periodized-and-Non-Periodized-Resistance.pdf); [Springer Medicine abstract](https://www.springermedicine.com/comparison-of-periodized-and-non-periodized-resistance-training-/22255106)
- [Evidence/Analysis] Stronger By Science re-analysis of Williams 2017: after adjusting for nested effects and funnel-plot asymmetry, the effect fell to d = 0.23 +/- 0.05 but stayed significant. Confound: periodized groups usually reach higher peak loads, so the studies may reflect peak intensity rather than periodization per se. The only pair of studies (Antretter et al.) matching volume, average intensity, and peak intensity found periodized training did not produce larger strength gains. — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=52473)
- [Evidence] Moesgaard et al. 2022 (Sports Medicine): 35 studies; pooled 1RM effect favoring periodization ES = 0.31 (95% CI 0.04-0.57). Subgroup analyses: the advantage held only in trained individuals, not in previously untrained people. Periodized training did not lead to greater hypertrophy. — [PubMed 35044672](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/); [Biolayne REPS summary](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-12/do-you-really-need-to-periodize-your-training/); [Examine summary](https://examine.com/conditions/muscle-size-and-strength/faq/are-periodized-training-programs-superior-for-increasing-muscle-size-and-strength)
- Caveat on Moesgaard: one summary describes included samples as "19 studies with beginners, 15 with exercisers and one with untrained exercisers," which sits awkwardly with the trained/untrained subgroup framing; verify in the full paper. — [Sci-Sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/effects-intensity-volume-periodization-strength-hypertrophy-238/)

**Linear vs undulating (daily DUP / weekly WUP)**
- [Evidence] Harries, Lubans & Callister 2015 (JSCR 29(4):1113-1125): 17 studies, 510 participants; no difference between linear and undulating periodization for upper- or lower-body strength. Authors flagged short study durations and participants' prior training history as confounders. — [PubMed 25268290](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25268290/); [full PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Systematic%20Review%20and%20Meta-analysis%20of%20Linear%20and%20Undulating%20Periodized%20Resistance%20Training%20Programs%20on%20Muscular%20Strength.pdf)
- [Evidence] Grgic et al. 2017 (PeerJ): 13 studies comparing LP and DUP for hypertrophy; pooled Cohen's d = -0.02 (95% CI -0.25 to 0.21), i.e., no difference; authors call for more research in trained individuals. The paper covers hypertrophy only, not strength. — [PeerJ 3695](https://peerj.com/articles/3695); [PMC5571788](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5571788)
- [Evidence] A 2016 meta-analysis of 25 studies (traditional vs undulating) found undulating beat traditional periodization for 1RM, ES = 0.22, with no significant hypertrophy difference (ES 0.32, CI -0.07 to 0.71). — [Record at lida.sport-iat.de](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4043930)

**Block vs DUP in trained populations**
- [Evidence] Gavanda et al. 2019, 47 adolescent American-football players, 12 weeks: both block and DUP produced significant gains in muscle mass, strength, jump and sprint with no between-group difference. — [Record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4055173)
- [Evidence] Painter et al. 2012, 31 Division I track & field athletes, 10 weeks block vs DUP: trends favored block for strength and RFD but no statistically significant differences; block had better gains per unit of training volume (a derived "efficiency" metric, not a direct outcome difference). — [Record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4026554)
- [Evidence] Sabido et al. 2018, 15 young male handball players (DUP n=9, block n=6), 8 weeks, volume- and intensity-matched: both improved squat and triple jump; DUP showed greater 9 m throwing-velocity gains. Very small sample; treat as preliminary. — [Kinesiology](https://ojs.srce.hr/kinesiology/article/view/6326)

**Definitions and typical structures**
- [Convention] Block periodization (Issurin-style): accumulation (longest block), transmutation/intensification, realization (shortest). Secondary sources give accumulation 2-6 weeks, transmutation up to ~4 weeks, realization 1-2 weeks; one powerlifting example uses 5 weeks accumulation / 4 weeks transmutation / 3 weeks realization. A Bondarchuk-method lecture summary argues transmutation should be ~3 weeks and realization no more than 2 weeks. Sources disagree on exact lengths. — [HPRC](https://www.hprc-online.org/physical-fitness/training-performance/plan-your-workouts-block-periodization); [EliteFTS block periodization in powerlifting](https://elitefts.com/blogs/powerlifting/block-periodization-in-the-sport-of-powerlifting); [Excelsior lecture summary](https://excelsiorgroup.co.uk/vladimir-issurin-block-periodisation-uksca-lecture/)
- [Convention] Conjugate (Westside): max-effort (ME) exercises rotate every 1-3 weeks (one source: every 3 weeks for intermediates, every week for elite lifters); dynamic-effort (DE) work uses submaximal loads, "typically around 50-70% of 1RM," lifted with maximal speed, organized in 3-week waves after which percentages reset; Simmons lists the dynamic method as a replacement for a second max-effort workout. — [Liftosaur Westside summary](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/westside-conjugate-method); [Westside Barbell: strength training methods](https://www.westside-barbell.com/blogs/the-blog/strength-training-methods); [ExRx Westside](https://exrx.net/WeightTraining/Powerlifting/Westside)
- [Critique/Convention] A PowerliftingToWin critical review argues against conjugate for raw lifters (specificity concerns). — [PowerliftingToWin: Why I Wouldn't Westside](https://www.powerliftingtowin.com/?p=1817)

### Inferences
- For an intermediate (trained) lifter, the evidence supports using *some* form of periodization over a static scheme, and the realistic benefit is small (d ~0.2-0.3), not transformative. The mechanism is probably that periodized plans get the lifter to heavier peak loads and manage fatigue, not any magic in the sequencing.
- DUP vs WUP vs linear-within-a-block is a matter of preference and schedule. For 3-day lifters, daily undulation (e.g., heavy/medium/light days) is the natural way to touch each lift often at varied intensities; for 4-6 day lifters, block or weekly undulation across sessions is just as defensible.
- Block periodization makes most sense when there is a date to peak for (meet, test week). Conjugate's constant exercise rotation is poorly matched to a raw intermediate who needs practice on the competition lifts; the critique above is a coaching opinion, but it aligns with the specificity principle.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full text of any meta-analysis; subgroup effect sizes beyond those quoted (e.g., Williams 2017's exact trained-vs-untrained split) are not confirmed.
- No meta-analysis directly pooling block vs DUP was found; only three small trials.
- Stronger By Science's "Research Spotlight: periodization style" page (likely reviewing a newer meta) could not be read, so no 2022-2025 LP/DUP/block synthesis beyond Moesgaard is reported.
- Issurin's own block durations and Simmons' exact DE percentages were not verified from primary texts.

## Key Question 2: Progression schemes with exact rules

### Takeaway
The exact rules below are program-author conventions; the only progression comparisons with controlled evidence are RPE-based vs percentage-based loading (small, non-significant edge to RPE in Helms 2018; no significant difference in Hickmott's 2022 meta-analysis, SMD 0.21) and velocity-loss thresholds (<=25% velocity loss favors strength, >25% favors hypertrophy). Pick one scheme per lift, apply its stall rule literally, and change schemes only when the stall rule has fired.

### Cited Findings

**Linear progression (add weight every session)** — [Convention]
- Starting Strength novice LP as summarized by a program calculator: add 5 lb every session to squat, bench and press; add 10 lb every session to deadlift while it advances. Stall trigger: fail all reps at the same weight three sessions in a row, then reset ~10%. — [FitnessVolt Starting Strength](https://fitnessvolt.com/rpe-training/programs/starting-strength/); [Starting Strength forum](https://startingstrength.com/resources/forum/general-programming/32292-programming-running-next-thread.html)
- Starting Strength's own "The Reset: Why and How" defines a reset as a 5-10% drop in training loads without changing the program. — [startingstrength.com](https://startingstrength.com/article/the-reset-why-and-how)
- A general beginner guide gives smaller upper-body jumps in kg: bench and overhead press 1.25-2.5 kg per session (approximately 2.5-5 lb). — [FitnessVolt beginners guide](https://fitnessvolt.com/powerlifting/guides/training-for-beginners-guide/)
- Stronger By Science novice tips: when you stall within a rep scheme, deload 10-15% and rebuild through progressively lower reps with more sets (4x8 -> 6x5 -> 6x3); if you stall on your 8-rep day before your 5- or 3-rep day, lower the weight about 15%, add a set to the stalled lifts, and resume adding weight. — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/making-your-novice-strength-training-routine-more-effective-two-quick-tips/)

**Double progression (reps within a range, then load)** — [Convention]
- Rule: fix a load, add reps each session until you hit the top of the range (e.g., 8-12) on all working sets with good form, then increase load and drop back to the bottom of the range. Starting load: one you can do for at least the bottom of the range with a couple of reps in reserve. — [Setgraph](https://setgraph.app/articles/double-progression-the-smarter-way-to-add-reps-and-weight); [FitBudd definition](https://www.fitbudd.com/academy/double-progression-definition-and-examples)
- Muscle & Strength Pyramid (Helms) program description distinguishes: *standard* double progression = increase weight once all sets surpass the rep range; *dynamic* double progression = each set is independent, and weight is increased on a specific set the following week only if that set surpassed the range. — [Boostcamp: Muscle & Strength Pyramid intermediate program](https://www.boostcamp.app/muscle-and-strength-pyramid/intermediate-bodybuilding-program)

**Triple progression (reps -> sets -> load)** — [Convention]
- Adds sets as a third step: add reps, then sets, then weight. The M&S Pyramid variant follows dynamic double progression but also requires surpassing the rep range by 3-4 high-quality reps before adding load. — [StrongFirst](https://www.strongfirst.com/?p=64439); [Muscle & Strength](https://www.muscleandstrength.com/node/958); [Boostcamp M&S Pyramid](https://www.boostcamp.app/muscle-and-strength-pyramid/intermediate-bodybuilding-program)

**5/3/1 wave loading (Wendler)** — [Convention]
- Training max (TM): original book uses 90% of true 1RM; Wendler's later "hardgainers" guide uses 85%, and he has said stronger lifters do better with a lower TM. Check: if you can't hit at least 5 reps at 95% (of TM), the TM is too high. — [JimWendler.com: 5/3/1 for Hardgainers](https://www.jimwendler.com/blogs/jimwendler-com/5-3-1-for-hardgainers); [T-Nation thread](https://t-nation.com/t/5-3-1-with-actual-maxes/177994); [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/5-3-1-workout)
- Weekly percentages of TM: Week 1: 65/75/85% x5/5/5+; Week 2: 70/80/90% x3/3/3+; Week 3: 75/85/95% x5/3/1+; Week 4 deload: 40/50/60% x5. "+" = AMRAP, but not to grinding failure. — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/5-3-1-workout); [5/3/1 calculator](https://www.calculate.co.nz/5-3-1-calculator.php)
- Progression: after each cycle add 5 lb to bench/press TM and 10 lb to squat/deadlift TM. Increases apply to the TM, not the true 1RM ("It's TM"; "you don't get to add 5 or 10lbs to your 1RM just because you finished 3 weeks of 5/3/1"). Wendler: "if any of your reps fail to adhere to these standards during your training cycle, YOU DO NOT INCREASE YOUR TRAINING MAX." No need to re-max each cycle. — [T-Nation: Adding weight with 531](https://t-nation.com/t/adding-weight-with-531/203422); [T-Nation: Progressing weight 1RM or TM](https://t-nation.com/t/progressing-weight-1rm-or-tm/222865); [JimWendler.com hardgainers](https://www.jimwendler.com/blogs/jimwendler-com/5-3-1-for-hardgainers)
- Stall rule: if AMRAP targets can't be beaten, reset that lift's TM to ~90% of its current value and rebuild. — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/5-3-1-workout)
- 5/3/1 Forever structure: "leader" templates (build work capacity) then "anchor" templates (express gains); a TM test / "7th week protocol" replaces the built-in deload: work up at 70/80/90% x5 then 100% TM for 3-5 reps; miss and you recalculate and drop the TM. (Reader-transcribed, not Wendler's text.) — [Boostcamp PR test](https://www.boostcamp.app/users/EtTagx-wendler-531-pr-test); [MyFitnessPal forum post](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10550293/5-3-1-forever)

**GZCL / GZCLP rep-max-based progression (Cody Lefever)** — [Convention]
- Tier structure: T1 main compound 85-100% intensity, ~10-15 total reps; T2 secondary compound 65-85%, 20-30 total reps (5-8 reps/set); T3 accessories <=65%, 30+ total reps; 1:2:3 ratio of T1:T2:T3 reps across the week. Described as "soft" gradients, not hard cutoffs. — [Bodybuilding.com GZCL](https://bodybuilding.com/fun/squat-bigger-with-the-gzcl-method.html); [Boostcamp GZCL methodology](https://www.boostcamp.app/methodology/gzcl); [FitnessVolt GZCL](https://fitnessvolt.com/gzcl-method/)
- GZCLP (linear beginner version): T1 starts 5x3 (last set AMRAP), add weight each session; when base volume of 15 reps is not achieved, move to 6x2, then 10x1; T1/T2 add 5 lb (bench/press) or 10 lb (squat/deadlift) per session; T2 runs 3x10 -> 3x8 -> 3x6 stages; T3 adds weight when the AMRAP set reaches 25 reps; after failing the last T1 stage, test a new 5RM and restart at 85% of it. Sources conflict on whether the "training max" is a 2RM or ~85% of a 5RM; the 5RM/85% version matches the reset rule. — [Set For Set GZCLP](https://www.setforset.com/blogs/news/gzclp-beginner-linear-progression-strength-program); [thefitness.wiki GZCLP](https://thefitness.wiki/routines/gzclp/); [Liftosaur GZCLP](https://liftosaur.com/programs/gzclp)

**Percentage-based cycles / Texas Method (weekly progression)** — [Convention]
- Texas Method: Monday volume day 5x5 at ~90% of Friday's 5RM (Starting Strength: ~10% below the Friday top set); Wednesday light day ~80% of Monday's load (sources disagree; one forum reading says 80% of 1RM); Friday intensity day a new 1x5 PR. Progression ~5 lb/week on squat and deadlift; bench and press rise about every two weeks because they alternate. If Friday PRs stall, reduce Monday volume rather than forcing the load. — [Starting Strength: Texas Method intensity day](https://startingstrength.com/article/understanding-the-texas-method-intensity-day); [FitnessVolt Texas Method](https://fitnessvolt.com/rpe-training/programs/texas-method/); [Liftosaur Texas Method](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/texasmethod)

**RPE / RIR-based load selection (Tuchscherer, Helms)**
- [Evidence] Helms et al. 2018 (Frontiers in Physiology): resistance-trained men (19-35 y), 8-week DUP squat/bench 3x/week; 1RM-percentage group (n=11) vs RPE group (n=10) choosing loads to hit target RPE ranges. Squat gains 17.05 kg (RPE) vs 13.91 kg (%); bench 10.70 kg vs 9.64 kg; differences not statistically significant; magnitude-based inference gave RPE a small advantage. Conclusion: both effective; RPE "may provide a small 1RM strength advantage in a majority of individuals." n=21, 8 weeks: suggestive only. — [PMC5877330](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5877330/); [PubMed 29628895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29628895/)
- [Evidence] Hickmott et al. 2022 (Sports Medicine - Open) meta-analysis, 15 studies (6 load-autoregulation, 9 volume-autoregulation) in trained people: autoregulated load (RPE or velocity) vs percentage-based 1RM difference MD = 2.07 kg (95% CI -0.32 to 4.46, p = 0.09, SMD = 0.21), not significant. No study had tested chronic effects of an "RPE stop" volume strategy. — [PMC8762534](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8762534/)
- [Evidence] Helms et al. 2020 review (J Human Kinetics, 90 studies): performance-based individualisation of load progression, flexible training configurations, and RIR-based RPE and velocity adjustments are "practical, reliable and show preliminary utility." Rationale for RIR-based RPE: the Borg scale yields submaximal scores (6.8-9.0) even at volitional failure. — [JHK](https://johk.pl/?p=5643)
- [Evidence] RIR accuracy is better close to failure and at higher loads; accuracy decreases as reps increase and %1RM decreases (Russo et al. 2025 systematic review; Helms powerlifter bench study). — [Russo 2025 PDF](https://dspace.uevora.pt/rdpc/bitstream/10174/39438/1/2025%20Russo%20et%20al.%20Factors%20influencing%20the%20accuracy%20of%20the%20repetition%20in%20reserve%20scale%20in%20resistance%20training%20%20a%20systematic%20review.pdf); [2024 scoping review PMC11127506](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11127506/)
- [Convention] Reactive Training Systems (Tuchscherer) fatigue-percent method: work up to a top set at RPE 9-10, then drop load by a fatigue percentage and repeat sets until that reduced load also hits the target RPE. Example: 300x3 @9, continue until 285x3 is a 9 = 5% fatigue. Stress tiers: low ~3% or less, medium ~5%, high ~7% or more; 5% is "fairly average" (405 -> ~385). — [EliteFTS: Reactive Training Manual overview](https://elitefts.com/education/a-reactive-training-manual-overview); [EliteFTS RPE chapter](https://elitefts.com/blogs/training/rate-of-perceived-exertion-rpe); [PowerliftingToWin RTS review](https://www.powerliftingtowin.com/?p=1096)
- [Convention] Barbell Medicine: compute e1RM each workout from the most accurate set available (e.g., 5 @ RPE 9 or a single @ RPE 8) and set back-off loads as a percentage of that day's e1RM instead of a stale tested max. — [Barbell Medicine forum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/e1rm-calculation-in-the-templates/664)

**Velocity-based training**
- [Evidence] Hickmott 2022: velocity-loss thresholds <=25% vs >25% gave a significant 1RM advantage (MD 2.32 kg, 95% CI 0.33-4.31, p = 0.02, SMD 0.23) but smaller CSA gains (MD 0.61 cm2, 95% CI 0.05-1.16, p = 0.03, SMD 0.28). — [PMC8762534](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8762534/)
- [Evidence] Zhang et al. 2023 dose-response meta-analysis: velocity loss of 20-30% may be best for maximal strength; lower loss is more efficient for developing/maintaining strength. — [PMC9914552](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9914552/)
- [Evidence] Jukic et al. 2023 (Sports Medicine) systematic review/meta-analysis of acute and chronic effects of velocity-loss thresholds; a secondhand summary reports strength gains equivalent at 10% vs 40% loss and each 10% more loss associated with more hypertrophy (beta = 0.006) - unverified against the paper. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s40279-022-01754-4); [secondhand summary](https://www.velouniversity.com/post/velocity-loss-and-fatigue-why-more-isnt-always-better-in-strength-training-for-baseball-performance)
- [Evidence] Pareja-Blanco 8-week squat trial: 20% vs 40% velocity loss produced similar squat strength gains; 20% group improved jumps more despite fewer reps. — [SimpliFaster summary](https://simplifaster.com/articles/using-velocity-loss-between-sets/)
- [Evidence] 2025 scoping review (25 studies): thresholds >=30-40% consistently raise fatigue and recovery demand; <=10-20% preserve velocity/power. — [Scoping review record](https://drepo.sdl.edu.sa/items/da2b9d4d-47ed-495d-86e2-dc26888e02f2)
- [Evidence] Weakley et al. 2020: 10/20/30% thresholds in back squat produce reliably different rep counts and kinetic outputs (reliability CV <10%). — [Weakley 2020 PDF](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/download/1d63960ee760a11862500b396fd4c7673346354ab7e6205fa0398a223efd2c91/243348/AM_Weakley_2020_The_effects_of_10_20_and.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a 3-6 day intermediate: use linear per-session progression only while it works (late-novice/early-intermediate); switch to weekly (Texas-Method-style) or cycle-based (5/3/1, GZCL) progression when per-session jumps fail twice in a row at a lift.
- Double progression suits accessories and hypertrophy work at 8-15 reps; triple progression is a sensible next step for 5-6 day lifters who have room to add sets.
- RPE-based loading is at least as good as percentages and more robust to daily variation; percentages are easier to execute and fine for a lifter still learning RPE. A hybrid (percentage targets with an RPE cap, or e1RM-derived back-offs) captures most of both.
- VBT without a device can be approximated by RIR: stopping sets at 2-4 RIR corresponds to the low-velocity-loss region favoring strength and power; grinding to 0-1 RIR corresponds to high velocity loss favoring hypertrophy at a fatigue cost.

### Gaps
- Tuchscherer's RPE-to-%1RM table values were not retrieved; a third-party converter listing ~76.2% for one RPE-9 row could not be verified.
- Helms' exact increment rules in the Muscle & Strength Pyramid (e.g., 2.5%/5% load jumps) were not found.
- Wendler's "5 forward, 3 back" rule and the exact 5/3/1 Forever TM-test scheme were not confirmed from his own text.
- Lefever's original GZCL blog post was not accessible; tier rules come from secondary program sites that disagree on the TM definition.
- No controlled trial compares double vs triple vs linear progression directly.

## Key Question 3: Deloads - evidence and expert consensus

### Takeaway
The only RCT (Coleman et al. 2024) found a full week off mid-block cost lower-body strength gains with no hypertrophy, power or endurance penalty, so complete rest is not a free lunch for strength; but that study did not test a volume-reduced deload. Coaching consensus (Bell 2022 interviews; Bell 2023 Delphi) is to deload about every 4-6 weeks for 5-7 days, cutting volume and effort while keeping frequency, with growing preference for flexible/reactive timing over fixed schedules.

### Cited Findings
- [Evidence] Coleman et al. 2024 (PeerJ): 39 resistance-trained participants (29 M, 10 F) randomized to one week of complete abstention from resistance training at the midpoint of a 9-week high-volume program (DELOAD) vs continuous training (TRAD). TRAD showed greater isometric and dynamic lower-body strength gains; no appreciable differences in lower-body muscle size, local endurance, or power; TRAD had slight readiness-to-train advantages. Authors: a 1-week deload at the midpoint appears to negatively influence lower-body strength but not hypertrophy, power or endurance. — [PeerJ 16777](https://peerj.com/articles/16777); [PMC10809978](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10809978/)
- Limitation noted by the authors/commentators: the deload was complete cessation; most practical deloads reduce volume/intensity/frequency instead, and the findings cannot be extrapolated to those. Detraining literature mostly uses 3+ week breaks, far longer than the 5-7 days lifters typically take; no direct evidence exists on whether deloads enhance later cycles. — [Look Great Naked (Schoenfeld)](https://www.lookgreatnaked.com/blog/are-deloads-useless/); [Biolayne REPS](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-14/the-first-ever-study-on-deloads/)
- Discrepancy: a Biolayne write-up describes an 8-week cycle while the paper says 9 weeks; trust the paper. — [Biolayne REPS](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-14/the-first-ever-study-on-deloads/)
- [Evidence] A separate 8-week study in beginners found no statistical disadvantage for a deload condition vs continuous training for muscle thickness or 10RM. — [Sci-Sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/active-deload-in-strength-training-does-temporarily-reducing-volume-hinder-progress-270/)
- [Convention/Qualitative evidence] Bell et al. 2022 (Frontiers in Sports and Active Living), interviews with 18 weightlifting/powerlifting/bodybuilding coaches: no single deload method; reductions in training volume and intensity of effort were the most-adjusted variables; typical duration 5-7 days, programmed every 4-6 weeks, though periodicity was highly variable. — [Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.1073223/full)
- [Convention/Consensus] Bell et al. 2023 Delphi (Sports Medicine - Open 9:87), 34/29/21 coaches over three rounds. Consensus definition: "a period of reduced training stress designed to mitigate physiological and psychological fatigue, promote recovery, and enhance preparedness for subsequent training." Volume, intensity of effort, and load are common adjustment targets, with less consensus on which to prioritize; typical duration one week; frequency usually maintained; deloads can be coach-, athlete-, or data-driven; 75% round-3 agreement that a deload can occur in the first week of a new mesocycle; two camps on proactive vs reactive. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0); [PMC10511399](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10511399/); [CoachLogik debrief](https://coachlogik.com/post/coachlogik-research-debrief-volume-1-issue-4)
- A later PeerJ paper citing the Delphi notes more coaches prefer a flexible deload approach over a pre-planned paradigm. — [Springer Delphi article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0)
- [Survey] Rogerson et al. 2024: cross-sectional survey of 246 strength/physique athletes on deloading practices (results not retrieved). — [TUS record](https://research.tus.ie/en/publications/deloading-practices-in-strength-and-physique-sports-a-cross-secti/)
- [Convention] Schoenfeld: the need for deloads "is going to vary substantially between individuals." — [Look Great Naked](https://www.lookgreatnaked.com/blog/are-deloads-useless/)
- [Convention] 5/3/1 built-in deload: week 4 at 40/50/60% of TM x5 (intensity and volume both cut). — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/5-3-1-workout)
- [Convention] RP-style deload as summarized by third parties: 1 week at ~50% volume with reduced intensity after each hypertrophy block; deload ratio 4:1 to 6:1 training weeks per deload week. — [Arvo RP summary](https://arvo.guru/resources/methods/rp-training); [skills.cat reference](https://skills.cat/skills/borisghidaglia/science-based-lifter/israetel-training)

### Inferences
- Evidence-based position: do not take a full week off mid-block if strength is the goal; if you deload, keep lifting (frequency maintained), cut sets roughly in half, and keep some heavy-but-easy work (e.g., top sets at RPE 6-7) so the strength expression doesn't detrain. This reconciles Coleman 2024 with the Delphi consensus.
- Reactive deloading (triggered by missed reps at a given RPE for two consecutive sessions, rising e1RM-adjusted RPE, persistent soreness or sleep disruption) fits an intermediate better than a fixed calendar, because intermediates' fatigue accumulation varies with life stress. A proactive deload every 4-6 weeks is a reasonable default for someone who won't monitor closely.
- Cutting volume rather than intensity preserves strength better in the short run (consistent with taper logic and with Coleman's finding that stopping entirely hurt strength).

### Gaps
- The Delphi's full statement tables (exact percentage reductions for volume/intensity) could not be retrieved.
- No study compares volume-only vs intensity-only vs full-rest deloads head-to-head in trained lifters.
- Stronger By Science / Milo Wolf's "Are Deload Weeks Hurting Your Gains?" article could not be read.
- Pritchard et al. 2015 taper review (volume reductions, duration) was not reachable in this session; taper numbers are not reported here.

## Key Question 4: Mesocycle structure - 4-6 week blocks, volume ramp (MEV to MRV), resets

### Takeaway
The RP model (coaching convention, not RCT-validated) starts a block near minimum effective volume and adds roughly one set per muscle per week over 4-6 weeks until performance stalls or volume nears maximum recoverable volume, then deloads about one week and restarts lower. Official RP chest landmarks for intermediates are roughly MEV 4-6, MAV 6-16, MRV 16-24 weekly sets; third-party per-muscle tables vary widely, so treat landmark numbers as starting hypotheses to calibrate individually.

### Cited Findings
- [Convention] Definitions (Israetel): MV = sets needed to maintain size; MEV = lowest set count that increases size; MAV = range where most growth occurs; MRV = upper recoverable limit. — [LiftVault summary of Israetel](https://liftvault.com/programs/bodybuilding/mike-israetel-5-week-hypertrophy-workout-routine-spreadsheet/)
- [Convention] RP's own chest guide (closest to an official source): intermediate landmarks roughly MEV 4-6, MAV 6-16, MRV 16-24 sets/week; "6-16 sets per week for most trainees (4-6 sets minimum for growth)"; beginners lower. — [RP Strength chest guide](https://rpstrength.com/chest-training-tips-hypertrophy/)
- [Convention, conflicting] An unofficial compilation of Israetel's "Training Landmarks Series" lists chest MV 8 / MEV 10 / MAV 12-20 / MRV 22+ at 1.5-3x frequency; quads MEV 8, MAV 12-18; back MEV 10, MRV 25+. A Boxrox summary of an Israetel video gives chest 12-20 sets/week. These conflict with the RP guide above. — [Scribd compilation](https://es.scribd.com/document/479545228); [Boxrox](https://www.boxrox.com/learn-the-best-way-to-train-the-chest-for-hypertrophy/)
- [Convention] Third-party general guides: MEV ~6-12, MAV ~12-20, MRV ~20-25+ sets per muscle per week; per-muscle examples chest MEV 6-8 / MRV 20-24, biceps MEV 4-6 / MRV 16-20. — [Arvo volume landmarks](https://arvo.guru/resources/methods/rp-training)
- [Convention] Block progression: start at MEV, add ~1 set per muscle per week in 4-6 week accumulation blocks; mesocycle length 4-8 weeks with 4-6 typical; deload 1 week at ~50% volume and reduced intensity; signals to end a block are near-zero RIR at prior loads and volume approaching MRV; autoregulate via soreness, pump, performance, and joint feedback. — [Arvo RP summary](https://arvo.guru/resources/methods/rp-training); [skills.cat](https://skills.cat/skills/borisghidaglia/science-based-lifter/israetel-training); [Boxrox: how long is the perfect program](https://www.boxrox.com/?p=202105)
- [Convention] A BarBend description: after week three, add weight and sets per session, from three up to five or six; scale back to three or four sets when fatigued or plateaued. — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/?p=260187)
- [Convention] Forum description of Israetel's program: sets escalate toward a final overreach week, followed by a deload (usually week 5). — [Barbell Medicine forum thread](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/deloads/10884)
- [Convention] 5/3/1 is a 4-week mesocycle (3 loading weeks + deload), TM rising 5/10 lb per cycle. — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/5-3-1-workout)
- [Convention] Block periodization examples: accumulation 2-6 weeks, transmutation up to 4, realization 1-2 (see Q1 sources). — [HPRC](https://www.hprc-online.org/physical-fitness/training-performance/plan-your-workouts-block-periodization)
- [Convention] Delphi consensus: 75% agreement that a deload can sit in the first week of a new mesocycle (i.e., "reset" weeks can double as the on-ramp to the next block). — [CoachLogik debrief](https://coachlogik.com/post/coachlogik-research-debrief-volume-1-issue-4)

### Inferences
- A practical intermediate template for a hypertrophy/strength block: Week 1 at MEV-ish volume (e.g., 8-10 hard sets per muscle, 3-4 RIR), add 1-2 sets per muscle per week and tighten to 1-2 RIR by week 4-5; deload week 5 or 6 at ~50% sets with loads kept at ~85-90% of the prior week's working loads; restart the next block 1-2 sets above the previous block's week-1 volume if the previous block produced PRs, otherwise at the same starting volume.
- For strength blocks the "volume ramp" inverts: hold weekly sets flat or slightly decreasing while intensity climbs (65-75% -> 80-90%), consistent with block periodization's accumulation -> intensification -> realization sequence.
- Because the landmark numbers conflict across sources, the robust rule is procedural: start lower than you think, add sets until recovery markers (sleep, joint pain, rep performance at fixed load) degrade, then deload. The absolute set counts are individual.

### Gaps
- No RCT directly tests "MEV-to-MRV ramping" vs flat volume across a mesocycle; the model is a synthesis of dose-response volume data plus coaching practice.
- RP's official per-muscle landmark table and the "Scientific Principles of Strength Training/Hypertrophy" text could not be accessed; the 3-to-1 RIR ramp often attributed to RP was not confirmed in any retrieved source.
- Optimal mesocycle length (4 vs 6 vs 8 weeks) has no direct comparative evidence that I could locate.

## Key Question 5: Handling plateaus and when to switch programs or exercises

### Takeaway
Research on plateaus is thin; the best-supported interventions are a modest load reset (5-15%) with a change in set/rep structure, changing the relative intensity zone, or reducing volume on the heaviest day when the top set stalls. Program-switching is contested ("program hopping"), and the sensible rule is to switch only after the program's own stall/reset rule has fired at least once without restoring progress.

### Cited Findings
- [Convention + limited evidence] Stronger By Science plateau article: changing programs is controversial and research is thin; one cited study found that switching relative intensity zones (alternating 12-week low-load and moderate-load blocks) helped subjects avoid a plateau. — [Stronger By Science: plateaus](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/plateaus/)
- [Convention] Stronger By Science novice tips: deload 10-15% and rebuild through 4x8 -> 6x5 -> 6x3; if the 8-rep day stalls first, cut ~15%, add a set, and continue. — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/making-your-novice-strength-training-routine-more-effective-two-quick-tips/)
- [Convention] Starting Strength reset: a 5-10% drop in load without changing the program; novice stall defined as failing the same weight three sessions in a row. — [Starting Strength: The Reset](https://startingstrength.com/article/the-reset-why-and-how); [Starting Strength forum](https://startingstrength.com/resources/forum/general-programming/32292-programming-running-next-thread.html)
- [Convention] Texas Method stall fix: reduce Monday volume rather than forcing the load; forum poster suggests holding volume-day weight 2-4 weeks before adding 5 lb so intensity-day numbers catch up. — [FitnessVolt Texas Method](https://fitnessvolt.com/rpe-training/programs/texas-method/); [Starting Strength forum](https://startingstrength.com/resources/forum/general-programming/39033-texas-method-stalling.html)
- [Convention] 5/3/1 stall: reset TM to ~90% of current; Wendler's TM test (5 reps at 100% TM) diagnoses an inflated TM. — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/5-3-1-workout); [MyFitnessPal forum](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10550293/5-3-1-forever)
- [Convention] GZCLP stall: move to the next T1 stage (5x3 -> 6x2 -> 10x1), then re-test 5RM and restart at 85%. — [thefitness.wiki GZCLP](https://thefitness.wiki/routines/gzclp/)
- [Convention] Barbell Medicine podcast position: "most of what gets called a stall is impatience with the noise floor at your current strength level"; a 2024 Sports Medicine analysis of 17 years of IPF data found no "kink" in the strength-progress curve by training age. — [Podcast listing](https://podcastrex.com/shows/barbell-medicine-podcast/progressive-loading-part-3-why-the-novice-intermediate-advanced-framework-doesnt-work-and-what-to-do-instead)
- [Convention] Westside rotates max-effort exercises every 1-3 weeks explicitly to avoid accommodation/plateaus; critics argue this sacrifices specificity for raw lifters. — [Liftosaur Westside](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/westside-conjugate-method); [PowerliftingToWin critique](https://www.powerliftingtowin.com/?p=1817)
- [Convention] Coach quote (Men's Health/Saladino): consider a program change every 4-16 weeks, only for a true program-caused plateau. — [AOL/Men's Health](https://www.aol.com/articles/want-stronger-without-wasting-time-192840224.html)

### Inferences
- Diagnostic order for a stalled lift: (1) check e1RM trend over 3-4 weeks rather than a single session; (2) check recovery inputs (sleep, calories, bodyweight trend); (3) apply the program's reset rule (5-15% load drop + rebuild); (4) change the rep/intensity zone for 4-8 weeks (e.g., move a stalled 5x5 to 4x8 or to heavier triples); (5) change the exercise variation (close variant: pause squat, tempo bench) for a block; (6) only then change the whole program.
- Volume reduction on the heaviest day is the classic intermediate fix when top sets stall; volume addition (another set) is the fix when top sets feel easy but reps don't climb.

### Gaps
- Exercise-variation trials (e.g., Fonseca 2014; Baz-Valle 2019 on randomized exercise selection) could not be searched in this session; no evidence on how often to rotate variations is reported here.
- No study defines a "plateau" operationally or tests program-switching vs persisting.

## Key Question 6: How beginners, intermediates, and advanced lifters differ (Practical Programming framework and critiques)

### Takeaway
Rippetoe/Baker's framework defines training advancement by how long the stress-recovery-adaptation cycle takes (novice: adapts between sessions, so add load every session; intermediate: adapts weekly, so plan weekly PRs; advanced: adapts over months, so use longer cycles), not by strength level. Barbell Medicine argues the framework is wrong about biology: adaptation rates do not change categorically with training age; what changes is remaining ceiling, measurement noise, and the stimulus required, so progression should be reactive to demonstrated performance rather than calendar-staged.

### Cited Findings
- [Convention] Rippetoe/Baker: novice programming progresses workout to workout; intermediate programming stretches progress to week to week (Practical Programming, 3rd ed.; intermediate section was the biggest update). The levels describe the time needed to recover from training, "not a trainee's strength or athleticism." — [Goodreads review of Practical Programming](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/20765602-practical-programming-for-strength-training); [JokerMag summary](https://jokermag.com/12-vital-strength-training-lessons-from-mark-rippetoe/)
- [Convention] Starting Strength article (Rippetoe, Baker, Bradford 2018): keep the novice logic of stress/recovery/adaptation while moving into "more complex stress/recovery/adaptation environments" for intermediate/advanced; forum discussion adds that recovery ability improves with advancement but not as fast as the stress needed for further adaptation. — [Starting Strength: Intermediate and Advanced Training](https://startingstrength.com/article/from-the-archives-intermediate-and-advanced-training)
- [Convention] Novice increments: +5 lb/session squat, bench, press; +10 lb/session deadlift; stall = 3 consecutive failures, reset 10%. Intermediate (Texas Method): ~+5 lb/week squat and deadlift; bench/press every two weeks. — [FitnessVolt Starting Strength](https://fitnessvolt.com/rpe-training/programs/starting-strength/); [Liftosaur Texas Method](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/texasmethod)
- [Convention] Andy Baker (per forum citation): run Texas Method 16-24 weeks before moving to a heavy-light-medium (HLM) structure; posters report stalling after switching to TM too early and returning to LP. — [Starting Strength forum](https://startingstrength.com/resources/forum/general-programming/45187-help-stalling-texas-method-2.html)
- [Critique] Goodreads reviewer doubts a novice can reach the book's example lift numbers on linear progression and finds the Texas Method's lack of isolation work surprising vs 5/3/1. — [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/20765602-practical-programming-for-strength-training)
- [Critique] Starting Strength Gyms themselves argue lifters wrongly copy novice methods into intermediate training and that the book's examples should guide rather than prescribe. — [Starting Strength: which program is better](https://startingstrength.com/training/which-program-is-better-texas-method-or-hlm)
- [Critique] Barbell Medicine, "Progressive Loading Part 3: The Death of the Novice-Intermediate-Advanced Framework": "all of the adaptations run at the same rate at year one and year ten"; the differences between lifters lie in remaining ceiling, measurement noise, and required stimulus; "the biology, the fundamentals of programming, how to progress" are the same. Modern programming's error is prescribing load increases on a schedule tied to stage rather than to demonstrated performance (reactive loading). — [Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/novice-intermediate-advanced-strength-training/)
- [Critique] Companion podcast (Feigenbaum & Baraki, May 2026) cites a 2024 Sports Medicine analysis of IPF data (17-year strength curve with no "kink"), a 2019 high-density EMG study, and a 2025 review on reciprocal inhibition; frames the stages as a "measurement window," not biology. — [Podcast listing](https://podcastrex.com/shows/barbell-medicine-podcast/progressive-loading-part-3-why-the-novice-intermediate-advanced-framework-doesnt-work-and-what-to-do-instead)
- [Evidence] Periodization's strength advantage appears only in trained lifters (Moesgaard 2022), consistent with the idea that untrained lifters progress on almost any progressive overload. — [PubMed 35044672](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/)
- [Evidence] Williams 2017 found training status moderated the periodization effect. — [Williams 2017 PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/Comparison-of-Periodized-and-Non-Periodized-Resistance.pdf)
- [Evidence] Harries 2015 flagged participants' prior training history as a confounder in LP vs UP comparisons. — [PubMed 25268290](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25268290/)

### Inferences
- The two camps agree operationally more than rhetorically: both say add load when performance shows you can, and lengthen the time between planned PR attempts as progress slows. The practical disagreement is whether to pre-assign cycle length by "stage" (Rippetoe) or to let the data choose (Barbell Medicine). For a self-coached intermediate, the reactive approach is safer: use per-session increments until two consecutive misses, then weekly, then 3-4 week cycles.
- Cycle-length rule of thumb consistent with both: novice 1 session; early intermediate 1 week; later intermediate 3-6 week mesocycles (5/3/1, GZCL, RP blocks); advanced 8-16 week macro-blocks with a peak.

### Gaps
- Practical Programming's own text (exact novice/intermediate/advanced definitions, Table of increments) was not accessible; definitions come from reviews and summaries.
- The 2024 Sports Medicine IPF analysis cited by Barbell Medicine was not retrieved, so its "no-kink" finding is reported secondhand.
- No direct Rippetoe/Baker rebuttal to the Barbell Medicine critique was found.

## Key Question 7: Tracking - what to log and how to judge whether a block worked

### Takeaway
Log load, reps, sets, and RPE/RIR for every working set and derive a per-lift estimated 1RM (e1RM) from the day's most accurate set; judge a block by the trend in e1RM at matched RPE (or reps at matched load) over 3-4 weeks, plus bodyweight and subjective readiness, rather than by single-session maxes.

### Cited Findings
- [Convention] Barbell Medicine: base e1RM on the most accurate data available - a higher-RPE rep set (e.g., 5 reps @ RPE 9) or a single @ RPE 8 - and recalculate each workout; back-off sets become a percentage of today's e1RM rather than of outdated test data. — [Barbell Medicine forum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/e1rm-calculation-in-the-templates/664); [related thread](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/rpe-and-new-e1rm/6441)
- [Convention] Progressive Rehab & Strength: a training log records exercises performed; tracking trends means metrics such as differential RPE (planned vs actual), average exercise RPE, and exercise e1RM, so progress can be monitored without maxing out regularly. — [PRS article](https://www.progressiverehabandstrength.com/articles/calculating-and-tracking-rpe-e1rm-for-barbell-strength-training)
- [Evidence] Helms 2020 review: performance-based individualisation of load progression and RIR-based RPE scores are practical and reliable monitoring methods; many physiological markers are lab-restricted. — [JHK](https://johk.pl/?p=5643)
- [Evidence] RIR ratings are most accurate near failure and at high loads, so e1RM estimates from heavy, low-rep, RPE >= 8 sets are more trustworthy than from long sets. — [Russo 2025](https://dspace.uevora.pt/rdpc/bitstream/10174/39438/1/2025%20Russo%20et%20al.%20Factors%20influencing%20the%20accuracy%20of%20the%20repetition%20in%20reserve%20scale%20in%20resistance%20training%20%20a%20systematic%20review.pdf)
- [Convention] 5/3/1's "+" sets are explicitly a rep-PR tracking tool: compare reps at the same TM percentage across cycles; failure to beat them is the trigger to reset the TM. — [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/5-3-1-workout)
- [Convention] RTS fatigue percents (3/5/7%) give a per-session dose record; stress level can be logged alongside top-set load. — [EliteFTS RTS overview](https://elitefts.com/education/a-reactive-training-manual-overview)
- [Convention] RP autoregulation inputs: soreness, pump, performance, and joint feedback adjust volume; rising RIR at the same load, non-clearing soreness, and flattening performance are deload signals. — [Arvo RP summary](https://arvo.guru/resources/methods/rp-training)
- [Convention] Coleman 2024 also measured readiness-to-train questionnaires, which favored the continuous group - a reminder that subjective readiness is trackable and informative. — [PeerJ 16777](https://peerj.com/articles/16777)
- [Convention] Barbell Medicine: much of what is called a stall is measurement noise at the current strength level, so judge progress over longer windows. — [Podcast listing](https://podcastrex.com/shows/barbell-medicine-podcast/progressive-loading-part-3-why-the-novice-intermediate-advanced-framework-doesnt-work-and-what-to-do-instead)

### Inferences
- Minimum log per set: exercise, load, reps, RPE (or RIR), and a note on technique/pain. Per session: bodyweight, sleep hours, a 1-5 readiness score. Per week: total hard sets per muscle group.
- Block success criteria (choose before the block starts): (a) e1RM on the main lifts up by any amount at matched RPE vs the block's week 1; or (b) more reps at the same load; or (c) same performance at lower RPE; for hypertrophy blocks, (d) measurable circumference/bodyweight change with stable or improved rep performance. If none of (a)-(c) occurred after a full block plus deload, apply the plateau procedure in Q5.
- Because single-session RPE is noisy, average e1RM over the last 3 sessions of a block and compare to the first 3.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed validation of the "differential RPE" or e1RM-trend method as a predictor of long-term progress was found.
- The claim that experienced lifters rate within 0.5 RPE of true effort came from a vendor page with no citation and is not reported as fact.
